Oh, look I earned a new badge for my contributions to my favorite online community! All the time I spent there posting useful information and helping other people finally "paid" off! I can now proudly present my badge on my profile page! What do you say? If I can buy something with that badge? If I earn actual money? No, come on, this is not how this works. Why does it have to be like that? Uhmmm...

Wait, let's take a step back a little and talk about online communities. They are wonderful places - you can discuss interesting topics with other like-minded people, you can get help with a question or problem you have, and you can get news about the topics you care about. The Web 2.0 gave birth to many platforms where communities can be easily built - we have Twitter, Reddit, Facebook and many others.

So, how does it work? A company creates a platform where user generated content can be posted. The platform provides the distribution, the users create the content. The users can get badges, Reddit Gold, leaderboard trophies, karma, likes and thumbs up, while the platform takes all the money from ads and subscriptions. Let's dive deep in some examples, so you can see in details, how ridiculous things are.

## Stack Overflow

Let's take Stack Overflow for example. People spend countless hours there answering questions, finding problems and explaining concepts to other community members. Stack Overflow is so popular among programmers for the help they get there that the Internet is full of [memes](https://www.reddit.com/r/ProgrammerHumor/comments/69uof3/programming_stack_overflow/) of it.

Let's look at one user called [U12-Forward](https://stackoverflow.com/users/8708364/u12-forward). They are at 1st place for the last 30 days for the Python tag (see [leaderboard](https://stackoverflow.com/tags/python/topusers)). U12-Forward has written 3,231 answers, 370 of which in the last 30 days. Let's assume they need 2 minutes per answer on average (this is probably too low judging by [some](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/68961796/how-do-i-melt-a-pandas-dataframe/68961797#68961797), [of](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/68919220/applying-getattr-on-python-built-in-functions/68919292#68919292), [his](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/59817473/sort-a-list-from-an-index-to-another-index-python/59817527#59817527), [answers](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/69117926/how-can-i-make-str-or-repr-return-current-class-name/69117956#69117956)). This means that they spent more than 100 hours in total (more than 12 this month) answering questions and helping people! Do you think Stack Overflow is paying him something?

Recently, Stack Overflow [introduced Collectives](https://stackoverflow.blog/2021/06/23/collectives-stack-overflow/). Collectives are communities focused around specific technology, like for example Google Cloud or the Go programming language:

> Collectives™ helps you find trusted answers faster, engage with product experts, and share knowledge around the technologies you use most.

Expert users that invest a lot of time to contribute to a certain collective get special treatment! They can earn... recognition And a Recognized Member badge! And if they put even more time in, they can see themselves climbing to the top of the weekly leaderboard! Are you excited to start "earning", too?

At the same time Stack Overflow is doing quite well financially. In June this year, they got [acquired by Prosus](https://www.prosus.com/news/prosus-to-acquire-stack-overflow-for-us18-billion/) for $1.8 billion. [Prosus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosus) is an investment company, so what they do is to buy and own other companies. Do you think they contribute to the Python community on Stack Overflow? I think so too. And how much money do you think U12-Forward got out of the $1.8 billion deal? You guessed it again, he got $0.

## Example - Reddit

Another place where very strong communities around specific topics form is Reddit. You can find a subreddit for basically every topic you can think of (and for many that you never thought exist) and it is the members of the community, the redditors, that contribute all the value.

Let's take a look at the [MachineLearning subreddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/) which has more than 2 million members and is a very active place to share news, ask questions and discuss the latest hot topics. One of the moderators is [cavedave](https://www.reddit.com/user/cavedave/) who contributes there and some other data science related subreddits. Now, cavedave has 279,750 karma, which means that 279,750 people found his posts and comments helpful and hit the upvote button. That's almost 300,000 people! Unfortunately, cavedave can't pay his rent using karma and is not paid in any way for moderating the subreddit or helping people in the community.

Another member of the MachineLearning subreddit, called [AsuharietYgvar](https://www.reddit.com/user/AsuharietYgvar/), [posted](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/p6hsoh/p_appleneuralhash2onnx_reverseengineered_apple/) about reverse engineering Apple's NeuralHash and shared the code. The post became very popular and AsuharietYgvar got several awards for it, including 2 Platinum and 5 Gold. Platinum and Gold awards can be given by Reddit premium users which pay $6 a month. Now, what can AsuharietYgvar do with all the newly earned gold and platinum? Can he go to a nice restaurant? Can he buy a new smartphone? Can he pay his monthly bills? You see where this is going, right... he can't! It turns out that Gold and Platinum rewards give you a virtual badge, a couple of days of ad-free browsing, access to a hidden channel and some more coins to give out.

So, let's recap - Reddit collects real money from premium users to get Reddit Gold. Premium users can give the gold to other users for outstanding contributions. Users that receive Gold get a badge. Right...

Not only that, but Reddit also puts adds in between the posts to earn more money. And these ads are expected to bring almost [$290 million revenue in 2021](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1260066/reddit-advertising-revenue/). In fact, the Reddit business is so valuable, that it recently got [valuated at almost $10 billion](https://www.theverge.com/2021/8/12/22621445/reddit-valuation-revenue-funding-round). And most of Reddit's money their majority shareholder Advance Publications - a multi-billion dollar private company owned by a super rich family. And guess how much of the money goes back to the redditors that create all the value? Again, $0!

## Twitter

You can't create a list of platforms used to create online communities and not mention Twitter. There were naturally formed communities on Twitter for a long time and now Twitter is helping bring even more structure with [Topics](https://help.twitter.com/en/using-twitter/follow-and-unfollow-topics) and [Communities](https://help.twitter.com/en/using-twitter/communities). I'm proud to count [myself](https://twitter.com/haltakov) as a part of the awesome Machine Learning community on Twitter. I've seen many people put a lot of effort to help other people get started with machine learning, discuss interesting topics, share great information and explain important concepts. Check out the [Machine Learning topic](https://twitter.com/i/topics/898661583827615744) to see what I'm talking about.

How does Twitter make money? Mostly from advertising - they had a revenue of [$3.2 billion from ads in 2021](https://www.investopedia.com/ask/answers/120114/how-does-twitter-twtr-make-money.asp) and additional $500 million form data licensing. But you need good tweets between which to put those ads. So this is how it looks like. But wait, do you think my friend [Santiago](https://twitter.com/svpino) got some money from this ad? I think my questions start to get too easy... no, he didn't. The money goes instead to the Twitter shareholders, who are mostly [rich, white, old people](https://www.usnews.com/news/national-news/articles/2021-03-15/who-owns-stocks-in-america-mostly-its-the-wealthy-and-white).

To be fair, Twitter is recently putting some effort to allow people to earn actual money on their platform like [tipping](https://help.twitter.com/en/using-twitter/tips), [ticketed spaces](https://help.twitter.com/en/using-twitter/spaces-ticketed), [Super Follows](https://blog.twitter.com/en_us/topics/product/2021/introducing-super-follows) and [paid newsletters by Revue](http://help.getrevue.co/en/articles/4754361-how-pricing-works-on-revue). However, these are all additional money streams and active users will not get a share of the core Twitter revenue.

## The Problem

There are many more similar examples. We seem to be used to the fact that we can only earn badges and points in most online communities, and we enjoy collecting those! Just as I was writing this, the perfect tweet by [Shaan Puri](https://twitter.com/ShaanVP) came in:

[https://twitter.com/ShaanVP/status/1449105084512542720](https://twitter.com/ShaanVP/status/1449105084512542720)

"People love earning points". But you know what people like earning even more? Actual money!

But how can this be implemented? How would Twitter know who should get how much from the ad revenue? How should Reddit decide who provided more value and deserves some real gold? This is not a trivial problem to solve especially using Web 2.0 technologies. And to be fair, the platforms deserve to earn money as well, because they also provide value (distribution) and costs to cover (servers, payroll). So now what?

## Let the users own a part of the community

What if we let the users of the platform own a small part of it? Think about how the incentives will be aligned then.

*   Users buy parts of a community they believe in.
*   Active users in the community get parts of it as a reward.
*   The user contributes to the community and creates value.
*   The value of the community grows.
*   The value of the user's share in the community grows.
*   The user can sell part of their ownership for real money.

Web 3.0 technologies like crypto tokens and NFTs actually give us everything we need to implement the ownership and the marketplace required for this to work!

I know this sounds like the "Bitcoin fixes this" meme, but in my next article I'll give a specific proposal how we can create a machine learning crypto community using these ideas.

And until then, let's go back earning some badges!
