Last week I did a small quiz about the Gambler's Fallacy 🎰

Most people got it right - the probability of getting heads after we got tails 4 times in a row is still 50%.



I find it interesting to compare two ways to look at this 👇 \n\n---\n\n Independent events

The key here is that every flip of the coin is independent of the others. The outcome of the previous flip doesn't have any influence on the next one.

In contrast, if you are pulling cards from a deck without returning them, then these are not independent. \n\n---\n\n In mathematical terms, we can write this like:

P(Y | X) = P(Y)

Here X and Y denote the outcome of one flip. You read this formula like:

The probability of getting Y given X is the same as the probability of getting Y - because X and Y are independent. \n\n---\n\n Getting tails 5 times in a row

You can ask, what is the probability of getting tails 5 times in a row and it's small - 3.1%.

If we denote P(T) as the probability to get tails (50%), then:

P(T,T,T,T,T) = P(T)P(T)P(T)P(T)P(T) = 0.03125

This is because each flip is independent. \n\n---\n\n Getting heads after 4 tails

The key difference now is that we are not asking how likely it is to get TTTTH. We are asking how likely is it to get H, *given* TTTT.

Mathematically, we can use the formula from above:

P(H | T,T,T,T) = P(H) = 50% \n\n---\n\n The lesson is that in statistics, it's very important how you formulate your problem, because you may get very different results

▪️What is the probability to get TTTT and then H?      3.1%

▪️What is the probability to get H given TTTT?           50.0%

The Gambler's Fallacy 🤷‍♂️ \n\n---\n\n One more note - this is different than the Monty Hall problem! The Gambler's Fallacy is a much simpler one coming directly from the different ways to look at independent events.
