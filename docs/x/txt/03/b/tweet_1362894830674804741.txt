How do you choose the kernel size in a CNN? 🤔

Choose 3×3 and go deeper...

Let me explain. Thread 👇  \n\n---\n\n Receptive field 📣👁️

The receptive field of a neuron in a CNN is the size of the original image that contributes to the input of the neuron.

In the first convolutional layer the receptive field equals the kernel size. Going deeper in the network, the receptive field increases. \n\n---\n\n Deeper instead of Wider ↕️ vs ↔️

You can replace a layer with 5×5 convolution filters with two layers of 3×3 convolutional filters.

Look at the image to see how the receptive field remains the same.

Taking 3 layers of 3×3 will give you a 7×7 receptive field and so on.  \n\n---\n\n Parameters count 🔡

The big advantage? Much less parameters!

▪️ 5×5 convolution will require 25 parameters.
▪️ Two 3×3 convolutions require 2*9 = 18 parameters.

This is a reduction of 28% 📉

For 7×7 the reduction is 45% and for 11×11 we save 70% of the parameters. \n\n---\n\n Different architectures 🏛️

AlexNet used fairly big convolutions (11×11), but after VGG in 2014 3×3 kernels became much more popular. 

Many of the problems of training very deep networks are solved nowadays, so this is a good option. \n\n---\n\n There are network architectures that try to combine different kernel sizes (Inception net) or create feature pyramids (Retina net), but 3×3 seems to work very well enough if you can use more layers... 🤷‍♂️
