What is the best Prisoner's Dilemma strategy? ♟

Robert Axelrod, professor at the University of Michigan, wanted to find this out in 1980.

He organized a tournament and invited well known game theory researchers to submit a program playing Prisoner's Dilemma. 

Thread 🧵  \n\n---\n\n The Rules 📜 

The rules of the tournament were simple.

▪️ Programs are paired to play against each other for 200 moves
▪️ Each program plays in a round robin against each other
▪️ Each program plays against itself
▪️ Each program plays against a random generator \n\n---\n\n The Winner 🏅 

Surprisingly, one of the simplest programs won the tournament - tit-for-tat.

Tit-for-tat always starts by cooperating and then repeats its opponent's move. \n\n---\n\n Why? 🤔 

Tit-for-tat can be beaten by strategies - like one that always defects. However, on average it performs very well!

In fact, tit-for-tat can never win, because it will never defect first! However, it will never lose by more than one move. 🤯 \n\n---\n\n So, tit-for-tat managed to beat much more complicated strategies submitted by over 60 other researchers from all over the world...
