The Curse of Knowledge 🏴‍☠️

This is another type of cognitive bias that prevents us seeing things from the position of others, who don't have the same knowledge as us.

To demonstrate it I asked you to put yourself in the shoes of a kindergarten child:  \n\n---\n\n The answer ✅

The correct approach is to "see" just the shapes of the numbers and don't interpret their meaning. Remember, the kids cannot read, but only count to 10!

Then just count the number of circles! The correct answer is 2, because the 8 has two circles. \n\n---\n\n The facepalm moment‍ 🤦

Many people struggled to see the answer (including myself when I first saw this 🤷‍♂️). 

The problem is that we are all used to reading numbers and it is hard to "unsee" them, so we can put ourselves in the shoes of the kids. Complex math won't help here... \n\n---\n\n Another example 🎨

Try to look at this image and quickly say out loud the color of each word, not the word itself. It is not easy, right? You just cannot not read the words!  \n\n---\n\n Watch out for the Curse of Knowledge 🔭

We need to be aware of this bias when communicating with other people that have less knowledge about a certain topic. We often unconsciously assume they know things that seem easy for us, but we forgot how difficult they were to learn. \n\n---\n\n How to avoid if? 🤔

When explaining something, try to consciously think about what the other person knows and try to adapt your explanation to that. Then you will be able to communicate your ideas more clearly and become a better teacher. It's easier said than done, though...
