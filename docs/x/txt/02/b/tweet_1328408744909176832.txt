Inattentional blindness 🙈

Sometimes when we are focused on a particular task, we  are likely to ignore unexpected sensory inputs. 

I asked you to play a game last week to demonstrate this phenomenon, so here is a more detailed explanation.

 \n\n---\n\n In real life  🏙️

If you think about it, you will find examples in real life as well. I'm sure it happened to you that you met somebody you know in a unfamiliar situation (for example a colleague from work somewhere in the city). It is likely that you "don't see" them... \n\n---\n\n Extreme case ⚠️

In 1995 an officer chasing a subject didn't see another officer shooting the wrong person. He was convicted for lying to protect his colleague. An experiment later showed that this was likely a case of inattentional blindness and he was probably not guilty. \n\n---\n\n Why it happens ⁉️

There are different theories, including limited congitive capacity, dominance of what we expect, saturation of attention etc. It is a limitation of our perception and attention system. \n\n---\n\n How to avoid it 🤔

Inattentional blindness may be difficult to avoid, since it happens if something is unexpected.

However, we can avoid distractions when doing important tasks, like for example looking at our phones when walking and especially when driving! \n\n---\n\n Further reading 📖
