How inefficient is web3? 🤔

Another common critique of blockchain technology is that it is inefficient and it destroys the planet.

Blockchains using Proof-of-Work consensus are very energy-intensive and this is a real problem. There are alternatives, though.

Thread 🧵

1/13 \n\n---\n\n If we don't want to trust a central authority to handle the data, we need a decentralized system that provides consensus. 

And creating this consensus has a very real cost. This is a trade-off we need to be aware of when deciding if we want decentralization or not.

2/13 \n\n---\n\n Proof-of-Work is a problem and the two most popular blockchains currently are using Proof-of-Work - Bitcoin and Ethereum 1.0. PoW requires a huge amount of mathematical computations to validate the chain.

Before discussing the alternatives, let's talk about some numbers.

3/13 \n\n---\n\n This website has a good overview of the annual energy consumption of different blockchains. Here are some of the numbers:

▪️ Bitcoin: around 130 TWh
▪️ Ethereum 1.0: 75 TWh
▪️ Polygon (@0xPolygon): &lt;0.001 TWh
▪️ Tezos (@tezos): &lt;0.0001 TWh



4/13 \n\n---\n\n @0xPolygon @tezos Now, to put this into perspective, an average US home consumes about 10 KWh per year. Let's convert the numbers:

▪️ Bitcoin: 13,000,000 households
▪️ Ethereum 1.0: 7,500,000 households
▪️ Polygon: 100 households
▪️ Tezos: 10 households

5/13 \n\n---\n\n @0xPolygon @tezos Wow, the difference is huge 🤯 (I just did the numbers myself and I'm surprised as well).

Even more, Tezos is not a small chain that nobody uses. It does roughly the same amount of transactions per day as Bitcoin (300,000) and about 25% of Ethereum (1,200,000).

6/13 \n\n---\n\n @0xPolygon @tezos Anybody that claims that the energy consumption of 10 average homes per year is too much energy to validate 300,000 transactions per day is not serious.

On the other hand, I agree with the arguments against the energy consumption of PoW chains

7/13 \n\n---\n\n @0xPolygon @tezos How do Polygon and Tezos manage to be so much more efficient than Bitcoin and Ethereum 1.0?

They use a different consensus mechanism, called Proof-of-Stake. Instead of doing lots of computations, PoS validators stake coins as proof that they are telling the truth.

8/13 \n\n---\n\n @0xPolygon @tezos In PoW, validators that attack the network waste energy which costs them money. In PoS, validators will lose their staked coins if they misbehave.

PoS is also more robust against 51% attacks because it is even less profitable than for PoW chains.

9/13 \n\n---\n\n @0xPolygon @tezos Currently, Ethereum uses Proof-of-Work, but there are plans to transition soon to Ethereum 2.0 which uses Proof-of-Stake.

To be honest, though, this plan is being postponed for several years now, so the word "soon" needs to be taken with a grain of salt.
