The Kernel Trick

The kernel trick is a cool method that helps efficiently solve some classes of machine learning problems and is used a lot with SVMs.

Here a short intuition without going deep into the math...

Thread 👇  \n\n---\n\n Imagine you have points belonging to 2 classes, which you can't separate by a line - this is basically what SVM does.

(Image source: Wikipedia)

However, you could do something here... 👇  \n\n---\n\n You can transform the points in a 3D space, where the 3rd coordinate is the distance of the point from the center (x^2 + y^2).

Now it is easy to draw a plane here and separate the points - problem solved!

(Image source: Wikipedia)

So far so good, but it gets better... 👇  \n\n---\n\n Transforming all points in another space may be computationally expensive - the Kernel Trick comes to the rescue!

It allows us to draw such decision boundaries in higher dimensional spaces (even infinite ones) without explicitly transforming the data!

How? 👇 \n\n---\n\n In SVMs we need to compute the dot product between points. There is a clever mathematical way to directly compute the dot product of the points in another space without explicitly transforming them!

We can apply a kernel that does that!

😲 👇 \n\n---\n\n Without kernel trick - explicit conversion ❌

1⃣ We have points 𝘅𝟭 and 𝘅𝟮
2⃣ We transform them into another space using junction 𝗳: 𝗳(𝘅𝟭), 𝗳(𝘅𝟮)
3⃣ We compute the dot product of the transformed points: 𝗳(𝘅𝟭) · 𝗳(𝘅𝟮)

👇 \n\n---\n\n With kernel trick - implicit conversion ✅

1⃣ We have points 𝘅𝟭 and 𝘅𝟮
2⃣ We want to implicitly transform them into another space using junction 𝗳
3⃣ We use the kernel 𝗸 of function f to directly compute the dot product of the transformed points: 𝗸(𝘅𝟭, 𝘅𝟮)

👇 \n\n---\n\n Coming up with the kernel of a specific function may not be trivial and requires some maths, but it is possible for many useful functions!

The kernel trick allows us to efficiently compute the dot product of points that we want to transform in another space, without doing the transformation explicitly.
